Note:
- Agile Frameworks: Scrum, Kanban, Lean, Kaizen, ITSM (ITILv4).
- Agile is an iterative and incremental approach to software delivery.
- Sprint: industry-wide adopted 2 weeks by default.
- MVP: minimum viable product.
- Waterfall is sequential (Predictive): Initiate Project > Requirements > Design > Develop/Plan > Implement
  - Variable Schedule and Cost, but Fixed Scope.
- Agile is a loop (Adaptive): Plan > Do Work > Check > Act > Start Over
  - Fixed Schedule and Cost, but Variable Scope.
- Agile Manifesto (DevOps is an option of Agile):
  - 4 core values:
    - Individuals and Interactions over Processes and Tools.
    - Comprehensive documentation.
    - Collaboration over contract negotiation (product owner).
    - Responding to change over following a plan.
  - 12 principles:
    - Early via continuous delivery.
    - Welcomes changes in requirements.
    - Deliver frequently.
    - Daily collaboration between Developers and Product Owners.
    - Motivated individuals and trust.
    - Face-to-face conversation (no silos).
    - Working software is the primary measure of success (the opposite of "move fast and break things").
    - Sustainable development.
    - Attention to technical excellence and good design.
    - Simplicity (the art of minimizing the amount of work).
    - Self-organized teams.
    - Regularly reflect on how to become more effective.
- Scrum:
  - Lightweight.
  - Transformational for organizations.
  - Simple to understand.
  - Difficult to master (scrum-master).
- Scrum Values:
  - People personally commit to the team's goal.
  - The team dares to do the right thing.
  - The team focuses on Sprint work.
  - Stakeholders (all) agree to be open about challenges.
  - Respect each other to be capable and independent.
- Scrum Team:
  - Cross-functional team members with all competencies to accomplish the work.
  - Maximize the opportunity for feedback.
- Scrum Ceremonies (Events):
  - Daily stand-up (yesterday, today, blockers).
  - Planning for future sprints.
  - Review/Demo of the past sprint to the product owner (customer).
  - Retrospective (lessons learned).
- Events At A Glance: Backlog > Sprint Planning > Sprint Backlog > Sprint > Incremented Product > Sprint Review > Sprint Retrospective > Backlog again.
- Kanban: Just-in-time delivery.
  - A pull system based on customer demand.
  - Board buckets: Backlog, In Progress, Peer Review, In Test, Done, Blocked.
- Lean: Value-added work only.
  - Eliminating waste, amplifying learning, deciding as late as possible, delivering as fast as possible, empowering the team, building integrity in, seeing the whole.
- Kaizen: "good change".
  - Eliminate waste and overly hard work, meaning work smarter.
- ITSM (ITILv4): for IT services.
- SAFe: Scaled Agile Framework.
  - Large Scrum for large projects.

Notes:
- Cultural organization: Apathetic (indifferent), Caring, Exacting (reverse of caring), Integrative (both).
- Culture of DevOps: Trust. Empowers engineers to learn freely, share responsibility and success, and eventually fail together.
- Modeling company culture:
  - Clan: collaborative, engagement is high, a desire for agreement and harmony.
  - Meritocracy: a type of culture where great ideas are prioritized, whether the idea comes from the CEO or the lowest level.
  - Holacracy: employees manage their work independently, with full autonomy, and the company structure is completely flat.
  - Traditional hierarchy: outdated and may cause employees to stop innovating and suggesting new ideas.
- Agile Mindset:
  - The law of the customer: an obsession with delivering more value steadily to customers.
  - The law of the small team: a presumption that all work be carried out by small self-organizing teams, working in short cycles, and focused on delivering value to customers.
  - The law of the network: the presumption that the organization operates as an interacting network of teams.
- Agile Mindset Characteristics:
  - Think in iterations
  - Collaborate
  - Embrace change
  - Focus on delivering value
  - Respect
  - Strive for excellence
- The Agile Environment: creates and supports a culture that encourages a team of people to work toward a common goal.
- Characteristics of an Agile Environment:
  - Leadership first.
  - Business value is delivered by small, co-located, cross-functional teams.
  - Team membership is stable over a longer period (~1y)
  - Management
  - Face-to-face
  - Transparency
  - Big projects are delivered as a series of small projects.
  - Frequent
  - Relationships and results bring joy.
  - Continuous improvements.
- Keeping a great Agile environment:
  - A shift of mindset.
  - A shift of skills.
  - A shift of behavior.
- Agile team: 5 to 8, self-organized, self-starters, supportive.
- Vision:
  - Inspirational and meant to unify people behind a single, focused idea.
  - Should reflect the principles of the founders but evolve.
  - Decisions based on long-term goals, even at the cost of short-term wins.
  - Must include: who you are, what you do, and where you need to go.

## Week 4

- Topics:
  - Phases 1 to 4: Plan, Code, Build, and Test.

- Required Reading:
  - [The Eight Phases of a DevOps Pipeline by JakobTheDev](https://medium.com/taptuit/the-eight-phases-of-a-devops-pipeline-fda53ec9bba)
  - [The 8 Phases of Continuous Integration & Continuous Delivery](https://www.densify.com/resources/continuous-integration-delivery-phases)

- Weekly Assignment 4:
  - List the tools, processes, and approaches from the first 4 stages of DevOps that are in use within M.
  - If your selected organization is currently not using any tools, processes, or approaches, please recommend some.
  - No more than 3 pages. You may create a table to illustrate your findings.

Notes:

- Planning:
  - Product team develops the product vision, roadmap, and requirements.
  - Development team prioritizes tasks, details/refines requirements for design specification.
  - Sprint Planning typically estimates effort (story points) and assigns based on capacity.
  - Tools: Jira, Rally, LeanKit, GitLab, Azure Boards.
- Coding:
  - Pull stories from the backlog and refine with peers about how to accomplish the requirements.
  - For main aspects: IDEs, Code Frameworks, Source Control, and Containers.
  - Containers (the class mixes VMs and CTs): Vagrant for VMs, Docker for CTs.
  - Frameworks: Ruby on Rails, Express, Laravel, Django, Spring, React, Angular, Vue, Svelte, Foundation.
  - IDE: VSCode, Sublime, Atom, IntelliJ, NetBeans.
  - Source Control: Git, CVS, SVN.
- Building:
  - This is where DevOps kicks in.
  - Developer submits a Pull Request or Merge Request, which triggers a workflow that builds and runs tests.
  - Build Pipelines: code is compiled (if necessary) and packaged into so-called artifacts (container image).
  - Testing: as part of the building process, some tests are also performed: Unit, Integration, Regression, System, Performance, User Acceptance.
  - Security Code Validation: static analysis (mostly), dynamic analysis (more complex and rare).
  - Tools: Jenkins, GitLab, GitHub Actions, CircleCI, TeamCity, Bamboo.
  - Testing tools: Snyk, Aqua, Sysdig, SonarQube, OVS.
- Testing:
  - TDD (Test-Driven Development): required to pass all checks before the build is considered valid.
  - Tests: Functional-based, Code-based (Structural Test), Specification-based.
  - Levels of Testing: Requirements Specification > Preliminary Design > Detailed Design > Coding > Unit Testing > Integration Testing > System Testing.
  - Functional Testing: End-to-End Testing.
  - Non-Functional Testing: Accessibility, Storage, Configuration, Compatibility, Installation, Recovery, Maintenance.
  - Unit: is the smallest testable part of an app.
  - Integration: modules are combined and tested as a group.
  - System: evaluates if the system is compliant with specified requirements.
  - Security: looks for code smells (known patterns that reveal security issues in code practices).
  - CVE: checks for vulnerable known packages, libraries, and other dependencies.
  - Tools: Selenium, Katalon Studio.

## Week 5

- Topics:
  - Phases 5 to 8: Release, Deploy, Operate, Monitor.

- Required Reading:
  - [The Eight Phases of a DevOps Pipeline by JakobTheDev](https://medium.com/taptuit/the-eight-phases-of-a-devops-pipeline-fda53ec9bba)
  - [The 8 Phases of Continuous Integration & Continuous Delivery](https://www.densify.com/resources/continuous-integration-delivery-phases)

- Weekly Assignment 5:
  - Create a table of the tools, processes, and approaches used by organization M for the last 4 phases.
  - You are encouraged to do additional research if needed or use the tools summary slides provided in this module.

Notes:

- Release
  - RM is a neutral facilitator between Dev and Ops.
  - RM leads the following areas:
    - Architectural complexity, interdependent and independent components, and dependencies.
    - Understanding of the whole infrastructure ecosystem, scalability, capacity, migrations, etc.
    - Document post-deployment/release lessons learned (retrospection) to simplify procedures.
    - Agile sprints, optimal resource usage, contingency/rollback preparation.
  - Sections:
    - Source Code Repo
    - Container Registry
    - Deployment Automation
- Deploy
  - The process of upgrading the release to servers in each stage.
  - Includes: traffic/user migration, testing, real user acceptance, non-functional requirements (NFRs) and feature validation.
  - Manages per environment: key and configuration.
- Operate
  - It is frequently combined with Monitoring.
  - Areas: 
    - Security - Vulnerability or weakness in code, environment, and operation.
      - DevSecOps - Bakes in security at every phase.
    - Container & Infrastructure - Manages the creation, deployment, destruction, and scaling of apps or systems.
    - Orchestration - Automates deployment, scaling, and management of workloads and services (e.g., K8s).
  - Tools: Sysdig, Falco, Black Duck, Bench, TUF, NeuVector, Docker Swarm, Pivotal, HashiCorp Nomad, UrbanCode, OpenShift, etc.
- Monitor
  - Collects and instruments everything in their DevOps environment to get control and visibility.
    - Gather and sort data.
    - Look for patterns.
    - Automate and remediate.
    - Assume worst-case.
  - Approaches:
    - Behavior-Driven Development (BDD) - deliver business features rather than technical features.
    - Requires observability, dependability, continuous testing, collecting NFRs, and delivering feedback.
  - Tools: AppDynamics, New Relic, Dynatrace, Splunk, Nagios, Prometheus, Datadog.
- DevOps lifecycle concepts that need to be defined inside the organization (note: they overlap):
  - Continuous Integration (CI)
  - Continuous Delivery (CD)
  - Continuous Deployment (CDp)
  - Continuous Feedback (CF)
  - Continuous Testing (CT)
  - Continuous Monitoring (CM)

  - [How to start DevOps: A step-by-step guide](https://searchitoperations.techtarget.com/tip/How-to-start-DevOps-A-step-by-step-guide)

Notes:

- Organizational Change
  - A constant force for alteration in the environment, structure, technology, or people.
  - An organizational reality that can provide opportunities or threats.
  - Involves rearranging priorities and leadership.
    - "If you want to change the culture, you will have to start by changing the organization" (Mary Douglas - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Douglas)
- Change Management vs Transformation
  - Assessing the past > Comparing it to the present > Determining the ideal future state.
  - Success measurement: how much better the future state is.
- DevOps in Digital Transformation
  - Cultural transformation.
  - Collaborative thought process.
  - Change in mindset.
  - Tools, technology, processes, governance, and leadership alone do not change those factors effectively.
- Change Management Principles
  - Is concerned with the people, structure, culture, and leadership.
  - Core Principles:
    - Understand Change
    - Plan Change
    - Implement Change
    - Communicate Change
  - Kotter's 8-Step Change Model:
    - Create Urgency
    - Form a Coalition
    - Create a Vision
    - Communicate
    - Remove Obstacles
    - Short-Term Wins
    - Build on the Change
    - Anchor Change
- Metrics
  - You cannot manage what you cannot measure.
  - Define what metrics are required to manage it.
  - Continuously create, collect, and analyse metrics.
  - Broad categories by:
    - People
      - Employee Satisfaction
      - Customer Satisfaction
      - Customer Usage
      - Number of Tickets
    - Process
      - Deployment Frequency (is it manual?)
      - Size of Deploys (can it be broken down?)
      - Deployment Length / Duration (is it manual?)
      - Defect Escape Rate (what and why were not captured in review, test, and QA?)
      - Recurring Failures (was a test written the first time a bug is fixed?)
      - Lead Time (how much time between starting and deploying to prod?)
      - Mean Time to Detection (MTTD)
      - Mean Time to Recovery (MTTR)
    - Technology
      - Automated Test Coverage (how much of your application is tested?)
      - Availability (what is the uptime and does it meet the SLA?)
      - Failed Deploys (how many deploys go bad and how many cause incidents?)
      - Error Rates (how many exceptions get thrown in production?)
      - Application Usage and Traffic (is an Application Performance Management tool being used?)
