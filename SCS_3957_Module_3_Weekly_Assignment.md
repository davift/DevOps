## Paper

### Vision Statement

- "To become the world's most trusted company, designing smart software for a smart world."

### Strengths

- Trust
  - While the vision uses the word "trust", in practice the organization's speech and its recent acquisitions targeted **market share** as the measure of success.
  - Although the vision should guide decisions even at the cost of short-term wins, in practice M focused exclusively on the next new product and neglected the stability, maintainability, and business continuity of its existing products. Thus, it was not aligned with its vision.
- Directional
  - Since it points in the direction the organization wants to go, it does not provide numerical criteria. This gives room to adapt as markets shift (a smart move).
- Memorable
  - Easy for employees to repeat and use as a decision filter.

### Weaknesses

- The vision is vague in answering:
  - "who you are" is not really mentioned,
  - "what you do" can be assumed to be a software company,
  - "where you need to go" only points to the future.
- It goes against the principle that "The more focused your vision, the more alignment you can expect to see from your organization."

### New Vision Statement

- "We are a global family of innovators who design, deliver, and continuously improve software and services that earn trust, so every person can rely on us".
- Why it works:
  - "who you are" is in "a global family of innovators",
  - "what you do" is in "design, deliver, and continuously improve software and services",
  - "where you need to go" is in "trust, so every person can rely on us," which also keeps the original emphasis on "trust".

### Conclusion

- While I focused on the Vision, I always considered the Culture alongside each assessment.
  - There was a clear dichotomy between the Vision and the Culture.
  - The misalignment caused conflict in decision making and consistently favored short-term wins as a means of fulfilling upper management's desires, not long-term goals.
  - While inspirational, the Vision did not resonate with the Culture and failed to "set the ambitious goals of the organization".
- I understand organizational culture as the collective behavior and intention of people, not necessarily the "vague inspirational phrases" from upper leadership, if that is not what people do.
